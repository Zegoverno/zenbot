# Close: keep what the job taught

A job isn't done when the work is: what it taught should make the next job better. Spend a minute,
not an hour; most jobs teach little, and that's fine. Save nothing just to have saved something.

## 1. What did the owner say that will matter again?

Preferences, decisions, corrections, facts about their projects → `remember` (source `owner` for
their words). Replace an entry rather than adding a near-duplicate. A correction of something you
believed wins: fix the old entry.

## 2. What did you learn about the world?

How a library, system or service actually behaves, why a decision was made, the state of a
project → `capture` to the wiki, with its source. Then make sure the page's summary is current.

## 3. What did you learn about doing this kind of work?

Only from evidence: a procedure that worked after a failed one, a step that was missing, a check
that caught a mistake.
- `find_skills` first. If a skill covers this work, improve it (`save_skill` with its name).
- Only if none does, and the work will come back, save a new skill: short, steps that end in
  something checkable, the reason being what happened here.
- A chore you did by hand twice, or a fragile command sequence → `save_tool`.

## 4. Leave things clean

No stray background processes, temporary files or half-done branches. Say what's left open.

## 5. Report

What was done and checked, what needs the owner first (decisions, approvals: new skill domains,
tools to approve), what you assumed, what you saved for next time (one line).
